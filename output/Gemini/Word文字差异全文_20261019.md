Abstract

Random passenger demand and changing road traffic disrupt bus headways, promote bunching, and increase passenger waiting time. Holding control regulates departure times, but its effectiveness depends on vehicle and stop characteristics, operating conditions, and the maximum permitted holding time. Reinforcement learning can select actions from operational observations, yet repeated trial-and-error learning on an operating fleet is impractical, and the resulting policy may encounter a distribution shift between training and deployment environments. This thesis studies sim-to-real bus holding control: robust learning under stationary stochastic conditions and adaptive learning under non-stationary stochastic conditions first address settings without pre-collected target-domain training data; cross-fidelity learning and model correction then incorporate target-domain offline data.

With passenger-time training, generalized passenger time increases by 0.068% to 0.268% relative to the preceding BAPR configuration, so the headway gains do not extend to passenger time.
