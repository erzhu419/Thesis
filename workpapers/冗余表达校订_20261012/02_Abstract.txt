[69 abstractentitle] Abstract
[70 abstractenfirst] Random passenger demand and changing road traffic disrupt bus headways, promote bunching, and increase passenger waiting time. Holding control regulates departure times, but its effectiveness depends on vehicle and stop characteristics, operating conditions, and the maximum permitted holding time. Reinforcement learning can select actions from operational observations, yet repeated trial-and-error learning on an operating fleet is impractical, and the resulting policy may encounter a distribution shift between training and deployment environments. This thesis studies sim-to-real bus holding control: robust learning under stationary stochastic conditions and adaptive learning under non-stationary stochastic conditions first address settings without pre-collected target-domain training data; cross-fidelity learning and model correction then incorporate target-domain offline data.
[71 abstractenbody] An event-driven controller selects the additional holding time after required passenger boarding and alighting have been completed. Its observations describe vehicles, stops, headways, and passenger-service conditions available at the decision time. Policies are trained using either a headway-based reward or a passenger-time-based reward. Multi-line experiments use an existing Changsha road network, bus routes, origin-destination (OD) demand, and timetables. An in-house simplified simulator supports low-cost training. Simulation of Urban MObility (SUMO) represents road traffic and vehicle interactions in greater detail and serves as a simulated proxy for the real operating environment. Cross-fidelity transfer combines SUMO offline data with new data collected through online interaction in the simplified simulator and evaluates the resulting policy in SUMO operating scenarios not used for training. In all bus-control experiments, the only control variable is additional holding time. The main contributions and results are as follows.
[72 abstractenbody] To address imbalanced training experience across vehicles and errors in action-value estimation under stochastic operations, this thesis develops a shared holding policy that combines categorical vehicle and stop information with continuous observations, and proposes Robust Ensemble Soft Actor-Critic (RE-SAC). Generalized passenger time cost is defined as twice waiting time plus onboard time. For service evaluation, the accumulated cost within the specified horizon is divided by the number of passengers who have entered the system; time already spent waiting or onboard is included even when a passenger’s trip remains incomplete. Under the same RE-SAC training settings, learned embeddings reduce per-passenger generalized time cost relative to one-hot encoding by 5.51%, 1.41%, and 4.91% under routine operations, demand surges, and rapid switching, respectively. In the multi-line experiment using the headway objective, RE-SAC reduces mean planned-headway deviation and forward–backward headway imbalance by 5.00 and 7.36 seconds, respectively, relative to Soft Actor-Critic (SAC).
[73 abstractenbody] (2) To address changes in passenger-demand and road-traffic statistics that reduce the relevance of historical experience, this thesis builds on Bayesian Amnesic Piecewise-Robust SAC (BAPR) to develop Causal-Symbolic BAPR (CS-BAPR). Recent rewards and disagreement among action-value estimates are used to adjust conservatism during training. Training stabilization is combined with a learnable baseline action and a neural correction, while soft symbolic constraints guide holding actions and their responses to changes in the state. Bus policies are evaluated with their trained parameters fixed. With generalized passenger time cost as the training objective, CS-BAPR reduces mean per-passenger generalized time cost by 0.083% to 0.549% relative to RE-SAC across six scenarios covering routine demand, single and repeated demand surges, sustained high demand, and brief extreme demand. Mean planned-headway deviation and forward-backward headway imbalance decrease by 7.35 to 12.09 seconds and 11.88 to 20.08 seconds, respectively. These results show average improvements over RE-SAC in passenger service and headway control under the tested demand changes.
[74 abstractenbody] (3) To use existing target-environment records without relying on continued trial-and-error data collection in that environment, this thesis establishes a bus-specific cross-fidelity training procedure based on Hybrid Offline-and-Online Reinforcement Learning (H2O) and its extension, H2O+. Policies are initialized using fixed SUMO offline data and then trained further with newly collected interactions from a fast bus simulator. The bus-control implementation handles simultaneous decision events and represents both the choice of no additional holding and continuous holding durations. Across five independent training runs using the same target offline data and matched numbers of policy- and value-network updates, hybrid training reduces mean per-passenger generalized time cost by 16.66 seconds under routine demand and 19.75 seconds under a demand level 1.25 times the baseline relative to the tested offline-only scheme. A trip-completion safeguard is also introduced for designated operating periods when a preceding bus is present, no following bus is en route, the onboard load does not exceed 25% of capacity, and serviceable passenger demand remains. Keeping the learned policy unchanged, increasing the minimum additional holding time from 20 to 60 seconds and extending the activation period reduce mean per-passenger generalized time cost by 92.12 seconds (3.71%) and increase trip completion by 0.466 percentage points in evaluations across ten new scenario groups and demand multipliers of 0.75, 1.00, and 1.25. The two comparisons separately establish the service improvements associated with hybrid training and the execution safeguard.
[75 abstractenbody] (4) To address prediction errors caused by insufficient source-simulator calibration during cross-fidelity training, this thesis proposes the Model-Corrected World Model (MC-WM). Fixed target-domain offline data are used to learn both residual corrections to source predictions and a direct target model. Prediction errors on validation data not used for model fitting determine which model generates one-step transitions for value learning. The bus model jointly predicts subsequent events, vehicle operating states, event intervals, and passenger costs. Normalized recursive confidence updates mitigate the progressive attenuation of model-sample weights. Joint source-parameter mismatches are introduced by changing road speeds, passenger OD demand, intersection delays, and required service times together. With generalized passenger time cost as the training objective, comparisons across 20 independent initializations show mean cost reductions of 51.14, 69.93, and 87.05 seconds relative to a hybrid-training baseline without a world model under calibrated, joint 50%, and joint 75% source-mismatch conditions, respectively, using the same execution rules. A separate comparison of complete controllers with the same number of source-simulator interactions and the same execution rules yields mean reductions of 23.02 seconds relative to H2O+ and 40.25 seconds relative to source-only SAC, with trip-completion increases of 0.158 and 0.263 percentage points, respectively. The results support the use of fixed target-domain offline data for model-assisted training to mitigate the effects of insufficient source calibration and improve passenger service under the tested conditions.
