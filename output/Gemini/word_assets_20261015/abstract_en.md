# Abstract

Random passenger-demand fluctuations and changing road conditions readily disrupt scheduled bus headways, induce bus bunching, and substantially increase passenger waiting time. Holding control adjusts departure times to restore balanced headways. However, most existing studies focus on terminals or a few key control points along a single circular route or unidirectional corridor. On circular routes, terminal holding is effectively equivalent to adjusting scheduled trip departures. Each vehicle makes only one or two decisions per circuit, separated by an entire circuit, so successive control actions are approximately independent.

This thesis investigates coordinated fleet control on scheduled urban bus routes with departures in both directions, allowing additional holding at every stop. Compared with conventional single-point control, this problem introduces three fundamental changes in decision structure. First, decisions increase from one or two per circuit to one per vehicle at every stop, producing tens of thousands of daily control steps. Second, upstream holding directly changes downstream passenger accumulation and following-vehicle arrival intervals, creating strong spatial and temporal coupling among control actions. Third, bidirectional scheduling and vehicle reuse across trips extend individual decision trajectories beyond single-trip boundaries. They also create large disparities in the experience collected by all-day vehicles and peak-period supplementary vehicles. High-dimensional operating states, strongly coupled actions, and imbalanced fleet experience complicate the application of conventional analytical optimization and fixed-gain feedback control. Deep reinforcement learning can adapt holding decisions to real-time operating states. However, operating fleets cannot accommodate repeated online trial and error with immature policies, so training must rely on simulation. Distribution shifts between simulation and the target operating environment consequently pose substantial reliability challenges for transferring learned policies from simulation to reality.

The research is organized according to the availability of target-environment operating data and the credibility of the source simulator. It first examines robust learning under stationary randomness and adaptive learning under nonstationary conditions without previously acquired target-environment operating data. It then investigates hybrid training across fidelity levels and world-model prediction correction using a fixed target-domain offline dataset. Decisions are triggered when a bus arrives at a stop and completes required passenger boarding and alighting. The observations combine categorical vehicle, stop, time-period, and direction attributes with continuous measurements of preceding and following headways, waiting passengers, and onboard loads. The controller selects an additional holding duration within the permitted range. Training rewards and service evaluation address deviations from scheduled headways and generalized passenger time cost. Experiments use a computationally inexpensive fast bus simulator and the microscopic traffic simulator SUMO (Simulation of Urban MObility). The former supports extensive training interactions and mechanism analysis, while the latter serves as a high-fidelity proxy for real operations in cross-environment transfer tests. The main research contributions and findings are as follows.

(1) For settings without target-environment data, where passenger-demand and road-condition statistics remain stationary but individual operating outcomes are random, this thesis proposes Robust Ensemble Soft Actor-Critic (RE-SAC). The method combines categorical feature embeddings with a policy shared across the fleet. During sequential decisions at successive stops, headway and arrival-time variances accumulate and increase throughout operations. This exposes bootstrapped value targets in Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) to environmental disturbances and causes systematic overestimation of unfamiliar actions in sparsely sampled regions. RE-SAC uses learnable embeddings for vehicles, stops, directions, and time periods to construct a fleet-wide policy with shared parameters. This addresses disparities in individual vehicles' training experience caused by bidirectional scheduling. A target-network weight-norm regularizer suppresses value-target shifts caused by random disturbances. Target-ensemble variance and online-ensemble disagreement penalties suppress action-value overestimation caused by insufficient sample coverage. In multi-line experiments with a headway objective, RE-SAC reduces network-wide mean scheduled-headway deviation and preceding-following headway imbalance by 5.00 seconds and 7.35 seconds, respectively, relative to SAC. Under identical RE-SAC training settings, learnable categorical embeddings reduce per-passenger generalized time cost relative to one-hot encoding. The reductions are 5.51%, 1.41%, and 4.91% under baseline demand (1.0 times demand), passenger-demand surges, and rapid operating-mode changes, respectively.

(2) For nonstationary changes in passenger-demand and road-condition statistics that reduce the representativeness of historical experience, this thesis proposes Causal-Symbolic BAPR (CS-BAPR). This adaptive method builds on Bayesian Amnesic Piecewise-Robust SAC (BAPR). RE-SAC's stationarity assumption treats all operating fluctuations as noise from the same distribution. In practice, operating-mode changes, including transitions between weekdays and holidays or demand following large events, have identifiable statistical characteristics. Once recent operating feedback identifies the current conditions, the mixed overall distribution can be separated into conditional distributions with substantially lower uncertainty. Accordingly, BAPR dynamically adjusts the strength of conservative evaluation during training using recent reward deviations and value-ensemble disagreement. CS-BAPR further addresses extreme demand changes that move operating states beyond the training distribution. Its policy output combines a learnable baseline action with a neural correction that vanishes at a reference state. Sparse symbolic identification provides a reference model of short-term cost. A soft symbolic Jacobian regularizer constrains the gradient of policy responses to changes in state. Across six passenger-demand scenarios with generalized passenger time cost as the training objective, CS-BAPR reduces mean per-passenger cost by 14.18 to 67.36 seconds relative to RE-SAC. These reductions correspond to 0.083% to 0.549%. For the same policies, mean scheduled-headway deviation decreases by 7.35 to 12.09 seconds, and preceding-following headway imbalance decreases by 11.88 to 20.08 seconds.

(3) For settings with fixed historical target-environment data and a source simulator calibrated sufficiently close to the target, this thesis develops hybrid training across fidelity levels. The method adapts Hybrid Offline-and-Online Reinforcement Learning (H2O) and its extension H2O+ to bus holding control. It addresses a common operational difficulty: operators possess historical data covering a limited period and a simplified simulator. They can neither conduct online trial and error with operating buses nor infer the actual target-environment consequences of untried actions from fixed historical data alone. The procedure first initializes the policy and value networks offline using implicit Q-learning on a fixed SUMO target-domain dataset. It then continuously collects online interactions in the calibrated fast bus simulator and updates the networks through mixed replay of target-domain and source-domain data. A sequentially conditioned state representation for groups of simultaneously arriving buses accommodates asynchronous multi-line arrival events. Five independent training comparisons use identical target-domain offline data and identical numbers of network updates. Relative to purely offline training, hybrid training reduces mean per-passenger generalized time cost by 16.66 seconds under baseline demand (1.0 times demand). Under high demand at 1.25 times the baseline, the corresponding reduction is 19.75 seconds.

(4) For insufficiently calibrated source simulators with persistent dynamics discrepancies from the target environment, this thesis proposes Model-Corrected World Model (MC-WM) assisted training. When source simulation is substantially mismatched, simply mixing source-domain and target-domain experience cannot correct inaccurate successor states already generated by the source simulator. Recalibrating microscopic simulation across the entire network is also costly. MC-WM uses fixed target-domain offline data to train both a residual correction to source predictions and a direct target prediction model. Prediction errors on a validation set excluded from model fitting determine the preferred prediction scheme for the bus-event world model. This model jointly predicts the size of the next event batch, vehicle operating states, the interval between events, and passenger time cost. It generates one-step predicted transitions to assist value updates. A normalized log-confidence recursion prevents excessive decay of the training weights assigned to model samples. Experiments consider normal source calibration and joint deviations of 50% and 75% across four types of operating parameters. Relative to the hybrid-training baseline without a world model, MC-WM reduces mean per-passenger generalized time cost by 51.14 seconds, 69.93 seconds, and 87.05 seconds, respectively. Service improvements from model correction increase substantially as source-simulator calibration discrepancies become larger.

Together, these four studies form a progressively developed methodological framework. Without target-domain data, robust value learning with two regularization terms counters random operating disturbances. Under changing operating statistics, recent statistical feedback and soft symbolic derivative constraints support policy adaptation. When target-domain offline data are available and source calibration is credible, hybrid training combines offline data with online simulation. When source calibration is insufficient, a corrected world model compensates for dynamics prediction errors. This framework provides a technical pathway from simulation training to practical urban bus holding control under different data and simulation conditions.

Keywords: bus holding control; robust reinforcement learning; distribution shift; sim-to-real transfer; world models
